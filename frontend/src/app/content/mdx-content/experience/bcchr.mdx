## Overview

At BCCHR, I worked in the AI and Data Science Unit on two projects supporting the institute's researchers through internal software and data science services.

My role combined software engineering, data analysis, and user experience design.

**Please note that only one of the projects is shown here due to pending confidentiality agreements.**

## STARFISH

STARFISH stands for **Self-management Techniques for Adolescents Recovering from Injury, Surgery or Hospitalization**. The project was in its design phase during my co-op, so the flows and architecture below were proposals that could still change.

**My Contributions**

- **UI/UX design**, including onboarding and authentication workflows
- **Translating researcher requirements** into technical specifications
- Early **React Native development**: a configuration-based onboarding form engine and local SQLite scaffolding
- Planning offline-first architecture: local database schemas, data flows, and service-layer interactions

### Problem

Adolescents recovering from injury, surgery, or hospitalization are often encouraged to practice wellness activities such as breathing exercises, meditation, and symptom monitoring. However, these resources are frequently disconnected from patients' daily routines, making it difficult to maintain engagement and build healthy habits outside of clinical settings.

STARFISH was planned as a centralized mobile platform that helps patients discover wellness activities, track their progress, and receive personalized recommendations throughout their recovery journey.

### Research & Requirements

Several key requirements emerged during the planning process:

- Patients should be able to access wellness activities as guests, with a path to creating an account and recovering their data later.
- Activities needed to be easy to discover, revisit, and save for later use.
- Progress tracking and daily check-ins should encourage habit formation without feeling overwhelming.
- The application should remain functional without a network connection.
- The interface needed to be accessible and approachable for adolescent users.

### Design

**Guest-first onboarding**

Patients can start right away without an account. The first onboarding screen offers two labeled paths, "Synced" and "on-device only", so the tradeoff is clear up front. Guests are told their data lives on the device and that deleting the app loses their progress, and they can create an account later from settings.

The confidential onboarding flow includes a researcher-designed questionnaire that personalizes suggested integrative medicine (IM) modalities for each user. I developed the configuration-based form engine that powers this workflow. Question text, field types, conditional logic, and response options are defined through configuration, allowing researchers to revise questionnaires without changing application code.

![Three onboarding screens: choosing a synced account or guest mode, a guest confirmation warning that deleting the app loses progress, and account creation](/images/adsu/starfish-1.png)

**Activity discovery**

Techniques are grouped into categories such as meditation and breathing, with search and a duration on each card. Patients can star a technique to pin it to the home page and return to it without browsing again.

![Three technique screens: all techniques with search, starred techniques, and a list of breathing techniques](/images/adsu/starfish-2.png)

**Ongoing engagement**

Recovery often spans weeks or months, so patients can set a goal, schedule, and reminders for each technique. The home screen shows their practice streak, and completing a session can add a new fish to their cove.

![Four screens: home with practice streak and daily check-in, a technique detail page, goal and reminder preferences, and a post-session reward screen](/images/adsu/starfish-3.png)

**Check-ins and progress tracking**

The daily check-in asks about body, mood, and sleep using three five-point face scales, so it takes only a few taps. Low scores lead to a matching technique, such as a breathing exercise after poor sleep, and a summary shows the week's trends. Check-ins were also intended to give researchers longitudinal data.

![Four daily check-in screens: body, mood, and sleep questions, a recommended technique, a completion message, and a weekly trends summary](/images/adsu/starfish-4.png)

### Technical Architecture

Alongside the user experience design, I contributed to the application's technical planning. The proposed local-first architecture supports offline use, improves responsiveness, and reduces privacy concerns for guest users, while leaving room for future cloud synchronization and analytics.

![Architecture flowchart showing local SQLite reads and writes, guest and authenticated sessions, and syncing to Django and PostgreSQL](/images/adsu/starfish-5.png)

## Reflection

Building the onboarding form engine taught me to separate research content from application behavior. Defining question text, response options, and conditional logic through configuration made questionnaire changes possible without rewriting the form's UI. This gave me a concrete approach to translating evolving researcher requirements into software that could accommodate later revisions.
